## The foundations the gaps share

- **F1 Scenarios.** A named, saved, versioned scenario separate from the operating plan: a set of assumption overrides plus proposed offerings, hires, sites and interventions, evaluated by the same engines the live pages use. Needed by 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7. Today the only way to test an expansion is to create a real offering.
- **F2 Constraint engine.** One evaluation that takes a scenario and returns every requirement (faculty, preceptors, learner-shifts by date × shift × setting, rooms, equipment, agreements, calendar) against supply, and names the first binding constraint and the secondary ones, with the date, shift or setting where each binds. Needed by 1, 2, 4, 7. The individual engines exist; the join does not.
- **F3 Cost model.** Cost by category (faculty, preceptors, coordinator, equipment, space, student support, partnership) with one-time vs recurring, lead time and useful life; cost per additional completer and placed worker. Needed by 1, 2, 4, 7. The schema holds no cost today beyond an occupation's wage band.
- **F4 Ownership and approval.** Owner, due date, status and escalation on exceptions, unplaced shifts and unconfirmed inputs; approval routing for large applies. Needed by 3, 5, and the proof-of-value measures of every use case.
- **F5 Assumption registry.** Every material assumption (rates, ratios, workload, lead times, costs, effects) with source, owner, date, verification status and a conservative/expected/optimistic range, versioned so a scenario can override it without touching the operating plan. Phase 3's evidence ladder covers clinical inputs; extend it to the rest.
- **F6 Shared identity across institutions.** One record per health system, site and preceptor pool, with each college's own agreement, assets and bookings hanging off it, and data-sharing permissions per college. Needed by 7 and by the regional half of 3.

## Recommended build order

1. **F1 + F2 + F5, delivered as use case 1.** The expansion analysis is the flagship decision, the one presidents and funders ask for first, and it forces the scenario model, the constraint engine and the assumption registry into existence. Deliver it as one screen: the question, the answer as a sentence with a date, the binding constraint, the ranked secondary constraints, the scenarios side by side, the executive summary and the operational trace.
2. **F4, delivered as use case 5.** Cheap on top of Phase 7 and it turns every list in Rosie into work with an owner.
3. **Use case 4** on the same scenario and constraint engine: qualifications, release time, hiring deadlines, the "lose this person" scenario.
4. **F3, delivered as use case 2.** Costs and the intervention library on top of the constraint engine; this is where "retention beats expansion" and "a coordinator beats equipment" become computable.
5. **Use case 3's remaining gaps**: locks, approval routing, scenario comparison, the partner-facing schedule.
6. **Use case 6**: licensure attempts, interventions, loss attribution, SIS reconciliation.
7. **F6, delivered as use case 7.** The only one nobody else can serve, and the one that needs the others to be true first: a regional commitment must trace to a feasible institutional plan.

## Numbers to hold ourselves to (from the proof-of-value lists)

Every phase should report at least one of these before it closes: time to complete an analysis, hidden constraints found, share of funding aimed at verified constraints, share of clinical shifts placed by deadline with named preceptors, staffing gaps found before the semester, critical exceptions resolved before the last safe date, forecast vs actual.
